About me

Engineer by degree, builder by habit.

I'm a Computer Engineer from UET Taxila with a genuine appetite for both halves of the stack — the interface people see, and the data underneath it.

Today I'm a full-time Software Developer at Aceturbo UK, working remotely from Lahore since July 2026. I look after their websites in core PHP — the front-end people see, the back-end behind it, and the databases underneath both — and I'm rebuilding their store as Turbo 2.0.

My front-end work started with an internship at Capriventures, a PITB-funded initiative under NEP-NICS, where I built responsive components, interactive navigation and forms that behaved consistently across browsers and devices. I moved to the other half of the stack as a back-end development intern at Hoboetech Pvt. In between, I spent a stretch as Sales & Accounts Manager at Agrovise — managing financial records, client databases and reporting. That detour taught me something most developers learn late: how the business actually reads the data we store.

My final-year project pushed in a different direction entirely — an EEG-based machine learning system that predicts how consumers respond to advertisements from raw brainwave data.

aceturbo.co.uk — Turbo 2.0

Home & finder. A rotating headline over a vertical slide show of cut-out parts, beside the reg, turbo-number and vehicle finder. The counters underneath read live catalogue figures.

Part scanner. Hover a part and the slide show stops. A HUD panel wires itself to the part, decodes its name letter by letter and types out what it does.

Part details. The part pop-up, rebuilt with every value escaped, a working thumbnail gallery and prices to the penny.

Admin sign-in. The split-screen sign-in for the admin panel. Errors now appear inline, where the old page raised an alert box.

Storefront & motion

  • Find a turbo by registration, turbo number or vehicle, through a five-step make → model → year → engine → power cascade
  • Nine cut-out turbo parts slide past the hero in assembly order. Hover one and a HUD panel scans it, decoding its name and typing out what it does
  • A line-art inline-four engine with a true slider-crank, firing 1-3-4-2, that revs as you scroll or type a reg
  • Shop, basket and checkout rebuilt with a vehicle hero carrying the reg plate, parts as cards on phones and a sticky order summary
  • One design system for every page: shared PHP partials, design tokens, self-hosted fonts and 30-plus interaction modules. Each one has a reduced-motion path and a no-JS fallback

Portal, admin & quality

  • A 13-page customer portal: split-screen sign-in, an order progress tracker, and case threads with drag-and-drop uploads
  • The AdminLTE 3 admin panel rethemed across 72 pages, with a live-figures dashboard and quick-link tiles
  • Safe for the legacy code: every id, field name and handler the old scripts rely on was kept, and edits were made with count-checked replacements
  • A PHP GD pipeline that cuts studio photos of parts out of their backgrounds as transparent WebP
  • Tested without touching live data, using a functional suite on shadow tables with a mail stub, a zero-dependency headless-Chrome harness and a link crawl

Final year project

Final Year Group Project

EEG-Based Advertisement Response Prediction System

A machine learning model that predicts consumer response to advertisements using EEG brainwave data — turning raw neural signal into a measurable read on whether an ad actually lands.

01 Objective

Build a predictive model that maps brainwave activity to consumer response, giving market research a signal that doesn't depend on self-reporting.

02 Implementation

Collected and processed brainwave data from participants viewing 50–60 second advertisements, then trained models on EEG responses correlated with user ratings on a 1–10 scale.

03 Impact

An innovative approach to market research and advertising effectiveness analysis — measuring attention and reaction at the source.
